    Shrine – spirit house

    San Phra Kan

    ศาลพระกาฬ

    A spirit shrine people revere, and home to Lopburi's resident monkeys

    An old shrine in the middle of town, revered by locals and the centre of the “Lopburi monkey” story — from the legend of Hanuman's descendants to the Chinese banquet for monkeys known across Thailand.

    02History

    San Phra Kan stands near Phra Prang Sam Yot and Prang Khaek in the old town and is a place of faith for locals and visitors. The sources I found do not state when it was built or the origin of the Kala spirit, so no unverifiable figures are given. For deeper history, ask the 4th Regional Office of Fine Arts or local authorities.

    The story passed down is that Lopburi has monkeys descended from Hanuman, tied to the Ramakian, in which Lopburi is named. Some writers note that earlier records do not describe monkeys as numerous as today, so the large troops seen daily are more a modern phenomenon than an ancient legend.

    03Architecture

    I found no verifiable architectural information on the shrine itself, so I do not describe the building. What is interesting is its setting, among Khmer monuments such as Phra Prang Sam Yot and Prang Khaek.

    04Highlights

    • The monkey banquet is held every year in late November; in 2021 it was scheduled for 28 Nov.
    • A survey of 4 main spots (Phra Prang Sam Yot, Manorah Market, Chayo Wanich shop and San Phra Kan) counted 3,121 monkeys in total, as reported by Thairath.
    • Monkeys are a protected wildlife species, so they are sterilised and health-checked to control the population

    05Before you go

    • Do not feed the monkeys yourself; leave it to the activities organised by the province
    • A few minutes' walk from Phra Prang Sam Yot, easy to combine into a walking route
