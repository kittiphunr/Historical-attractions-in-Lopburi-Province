    Baan Wichayen (Phaulkon's House)

    บ้านวิชาเยนทร์

    Home of Okya Wichayen, when Lopburi was the gateway to Europe

    A Western-influenced complex that tells the story of Constantine Phaulkon, a Greek official who was among the most powerful men of King Narai's reign, and the network of contacts with Europe at the time.

    Location
    Wichayen Road, Tha Hin
    Open
    Wed–Sun 07.00–17.00 *
    Person

    02History

    Constantine Phaulkon, or Okya Wichayen, was a Greek official who served in King Narai's reign and played a key role in negotiations with Western powers. His life and role are still viewed differently by historians and by relatives concerned.

    Silpa-Wattanatham magazine reported the discovery of Phaulkon's treasure in a buried jar, including a silver incense vessel in the shape of a Chinese junk and silver candlesticks used in Christian rites — antiquities more than 300 years old.

    Nearby is Wat San Paolo, which appears as an observatory and church, said to stand on land King Narai granted, reflecting that Lopburi was a meeting place for clergy and merchants of many nations.

    03Architecture

    A brick building in a European–Thai mix, with arched doors and windows and thick brick walls unlike contemporary Thai houses. The parts used as a church and observatory reflect the science and religion that arrived with French missionaries.

    Note: the layout and condition of each building have been restored several times; read the Fine Arts Department's signs on site as well.

    04Highlights

    • The story of Phaulkon, a foreign official still debated in Thai history
    • The silver Chinese-junk vessel, an offering piece that was unearthed
    • The observatory and church of Wat San Paolo nearby
